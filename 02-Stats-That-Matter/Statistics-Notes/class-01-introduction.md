# Class 1: Introduction to Statistics & Data Fundamentals

## 1. Why Statistics Matters in Data Science
* Statistics is essential because it helps us truly understand and interpret data rather than just process it.
* It enables us to summarize and visualize messy datasets, make reliable decisions using hypothesis testing and confidence intervals, and select meaningful features through correlation and ANOVA.
* It forms the mathematical foundation for machine learning, deep learning, and data mining, helping us avoid confusing random noise with real patterns.

---

## 2. Data Fundamentals & Tables
* **Definition of Data:** Data is simply defined as a set of information.
* **Data Table:** A tabular representation of information consisting of rows and columns.
* **One-Way Table Structure:** In simple tables, rows typically represent individuals (e.g., names) and columns represent variables.
* **Types of Variables:**
  * **Quantitative Variables:** Continuous numeric data such as Age, Salary, and Height.
  * **Qualitative Variables:** Categorical data such as Gender, Genre, and Place.

---

## 3. Data Visualization Frameworks
* **Bar Graph:** Utilizes rectangular bars based on frequency counts to quickly identify highest and lowest values without manually counting raw rows.
* **Pie Chart:** A circle divided into proportional slices, representing the percentage occurrence of each category out of the total.
* **Line Graph:** Connects data points with lines to track continuous trends over time, such as growth, drops, or stability.
* **O-Gives (Cumulative Frequency Graphs):** 
  * *Less-than O-Give:* Cumulative frequency plotted up to the upper class limit; curves upward from left to right.
  * *More-than O-Give:* Cumulative frequency plotted from the lower class limit; curves downward.
* **Two-Way Table:** Breaks down data to show the relationship between two variables simultaneously across multiple categories or years.
* **Dot Plot:** Functions similarly to a bar graph, but uses stacked dots instead of solid bars to display frequency distributions.
* **Relative Frequency Table:** Shows how often each value occurs in proportion to the total as fractions, decimals, or percentages, calculated column-wise or row-wise using the formula: `(Frequency / Total) * 100`.
* **Joint Distribution Table:** Compares two different distributions together (e.g., student category and traveling mode) to find the probability of both variables happening simultaneously.
* **Histogram:** A specialized bar graph for continuous numerical data that groups values into numeric ranges called intervals, buckets, or bins to evaluate distribution and skewness.
