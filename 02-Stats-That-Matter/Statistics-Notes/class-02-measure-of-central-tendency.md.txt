# Class 2: Measure of Central Tendency

## 1. What is Measure of Central Tendency?
* When working with data, looking at every value individually does not give a clear idea about the dataset[cite: 7, 8].
* Measure of central tendency helps us find one representative value that describes the center or typical value of the data[cite: 7, 8].
* The three main measures are Mean, Median, and Mode, each providing a different type of center useful in different situations[cite: 7, 8].

---

## 2. Mean (Average)
* **Definition:** Mean is the average of all values in a dataset, calculated by adding all values together and dividing by the total number of values[cite: 7, 8].
* **Formula:** `Mean = Sum of all values / Number of values`[cite: 7, 8]
* **Example:** For data `10, 20, 30, 40, 50`, the sum is 150 and the count is 5, resulting in a Mean of 30[cite: 7, 8].
* **Key Characteristics:** Uses all values in the dataset and is easy to calculate, but is **highly affected by outliers**[cite: 8].
* **Outlier Effect Example:** In data `10, 20, 30, 40, 500`, the mean becomes 120, which fails to represent most values correctly, making the mean unsuitable for skewed data[cite: 8].

---

## 3. Median (Middle Value)
* **Definition:** Median is the middle value when data is arranged in ascending or descending order; data **must** be sorted first[cite: 8].
* **Case 1: Odd Number of Values:** For data `5, 7, 8, 12, 15`, the middle value is `8`[cite: 7, 8].
* **Case 2: Even Number of Values:** For data `5, 7, 8, 12`, the median is the average of the two middle values (7 and 8), resulting in `7.5`[cite: 7, 8].
* **Importance & Use Cases:** It is not affected by outliers, making it ideal for skewed data, income analysis, salary data, and house prices to give a realistic center[cite: 8].

---

## 4. Mode (Most Frequent Value)
* **Definition:** Mode is the value or category that appears most frequently in a dataset[cite: 7, 8].
* **Special Cases of Mode:**
  * **No Mode:** When every value appears only once with no repetition (e.g., `2, 4, 6, 8`)[cite: 7, 8].
  * **Bimodal:** When two numbers repeat equally often (e.g., `2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5` where 2 and 3 appear twice)[cite: 7, 8].
  * **Multimodal:** When more than two values repeat equally (e.g., `2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4`)[cite: 7, 8].
* **Usefulness:** Highly useful for categorical data to find the most popular items, such as the most sold product, most common shoe size, or most common category[cite: 8].
